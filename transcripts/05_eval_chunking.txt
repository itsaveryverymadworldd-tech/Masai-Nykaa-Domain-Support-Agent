RECOMMENDATION (2-3 sentences, citing the numbers above):

  I would deploy B/sentence, the sentence-based strategy. It scores precision 0.5556 / recall 1.0000 against the fixed-overlap strategy's 0.4722 / 1.0000, which is the stronger profile overall.
  The reason is structural rather than accidental: fixed windows cut through sentences, so a chunk can contain the tail of one policy claim and the head of the next, which dilutes its embedding and pushes it toward whichever query happens to match the dominant half. Sentence chunks never split a claim, so each vector represents one complete policy statement -- and on a 12-document policy KB, where a single sentence like 'unless the item arrived damaged' can invert the meaning of the sentence before it, that matters more than uniform chunk sizes. Sentence chunks also produced a cleaner separation in Task 4 (out-of-scope top-1 max 0.1497 vs 0.1074), leaving a wider safety margin around the refusal threshold.
